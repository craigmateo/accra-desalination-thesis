# Abstract

The urban area of Accra, Ghana faces a number of imminent challenges related to meeting infrastructure demands. The issue of water supply for a growing population is of particular concern. Given the water-energy nexus, the question of water supply cannot be addressed without also considering energy infrastructure needed to treat, pump, and transport water. This study takes a systems analysis approach to the design and management of water and energy infrastructure in the Accra urban area. Optimization methods are used to determine the type of engineering activity required to meet forecasted demand, while system features are analyzed to determine details of scale, process, and cost. The identified solution (cogeneration of water and power using seawater desalination) is then analyzed in a broader water policy context to determine the degree to which it would meet assessed needs.
